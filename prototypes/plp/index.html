How to choose a bike carrier

We have a wide range of roof-mounting, rear-mounting and tow bar-mounting bike carriers. So how do you decide which one to buy? To a large extent this is determined by (1) the number of bikes to be carried, and (2) which bike carriers fit your car. Once you have decided which type to go for, you need to consider style, features and price.

  • You can fit a tow bar bike carrier to any car that can carry a tow ball, and roof-fitting bike carriers to any car with a set of roof bars. But all “back of car” bike carriers are vehicle specific.
  • If you need to carry more than four bikes, you will need a combination of roof-mounted carriers and either a tow bar or a rear-mounted carrier (if permitted for your vehicle).
  • Many people fit a tow ball specifically for carrying bikes. Tow ball carriers will take up to four bikes, but some vehicles are restricted to a maximum of three — your handbook will include the tow bar nose weight.
  • You can usually carry bikes on the roof unless you have a soft top, a “moon roof”, or a car where the roof bars are too close together or too far apart to hold bike carriers safely.

Our Buying Guide has more on the different types, including e-bikes and children’s bikes. If you also need roof bars, a package deal is often the simplest way to buy a roof-mounted carrier and the bars together.

Fitting & roof bars

Roof-mounting carriers need a set of roof bars. There are three types of roof mount — frame holder, fork holder, and tyre holder — and most will fit standard 32mm by 22mm roof bars, or slotted aluminium aerobars. One roof-mounting carrier is required for each bike.

Tow bar carriers fit a standard 50mm tow ball, including detachable tow balls. There are two types of tow ball — “bolt on” (sometimes known as a two-bolt flange) and “swan neck” (which includes detachable tow balls). Some carriers only fit one type or the other, so check before you buy.

  • If you use a tow bar cycle rack, you must not exceed the maximum permitted tow ball nose weight, taking into account the weight of the carrier and the weight of the bikes.
  • Most wheel-support tow bar racks tilt, even with the bikes in place, so you can open the tailgate. This is very handy, and sometimes almost essential.
  • Cheaper tow bar racks are “hang on” racks — you hang the bike from its crossbar. Wheel-support racks stand the bikes on their wheels and always come with a lighting and number plate unit.
  • All rear-door mounting cycle carriers are car specific. The cheaper “strap on” carriers are relatively cheap and can carry up to 3 bikes. On most vehicles the carrier can be left on when the boot is opened, although the bikes should be taken off first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I carry an e-bike on a bike rack?

E-bikes are usually better on a wheel-supported tow ball or hitch rack than on the roof. Use the E-bike compatible filter, and check the product page before you buy.

Do I need roof bars for a roof-mounted bike rack?

Yes. Roof-mounted carriers sit on your roof bars, and you will need one carrier for each bike.

What is the difference between a tow ball and a hitch bike rack?

A tow ball rack fits a standard 50mm tow ball. A hitch rack slides into a 50mm square hitch receiver, so it needs a hitch receiver, not just a tow ball.

Are rear-mounted bike racks suitable for every vehicle?

No. Rear-mounted carriers are vehicle specific, and boot access can be tricky. Check the product page before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

Can I carry my e-bike on a bike carrier?

It is nearly always possible to carry an e-bike on a bike carrier, but this will usually need to be on a wheel-supported tow bar mounted rack. Their frame shape is rarely suitable for the hang-on type of tow bar rack (which are anyway usually weight-rated to a maximum 15kg per bike). E-bikes are usually either too heavy or too long for roof-mounted carriers, regardless of the effort required to lift an e-bike onto a vehicle roof. Check the specifications on the product page for weight, tyre width and wheelbase, and always remove the battery from the bike before fitting it to a carrier.

Do I need roof bars for a roof-mounted bike carrier?

Yes. Roof-mounting carriers require roof bars. Most will fit standard 32mm by 22mm roof bars, or slotted aluminium aerobars. If you also need bars, a package deal includes the carrier and the bars together.

Do I need a tow bar for a tow bar bike carrier?

Yes. Tow bar carriers fit a standard 50mm tow ball, including detachable tow balls. Check whether your tow ball is a “bolt on” or “swan neck” type, because some carriers only fit one or the other. Do not exceed the maximum permitted tow ball nose weight, including the weight of the carrier and the bikes.

Are rear-mounting bike carriers suitable for every vehicle?

No. All “back of car” / rear-door mounting cycle carriers are vehicle specific — they are not suitable for every vehicle. Check the application notes on the product page. Boot access can also be tricky: on most vehicles the carrier can be left on when the boot is opened, but the bikes should be taken off first.

Can I open the boot with bikes loaded?

On a tow bar carrier, choose a tilting (or slide-away) model — most wheel-support racks tilt, even with the bikes in place, so you can open the tailgate. On a rear-mounting carrier, the carrier can often be left on when the boot is opened, but the bikes should be taken off first to avoid damaging the tailgate hinges.
