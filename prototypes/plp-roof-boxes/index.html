How to choose a roof box

Start by choosing the size you need, then use the filters to narrow the list — for example by length, width, height or colour. Most boxes are available in black or in silver.

  • If you want to carry other things on the roof as well as the box — now or later — choose a box that leaves space on the roof bars. Narrow or medium-width boxes will suit people who want the flexibility to carry bikes, windsurfers or kayaks. Choose roof bars that are long enough too.
  • If you need to carry long items over 2 metres, such as skis, you will need a long box, whatever the width.
  • Even if you don’t have particularly long things to carry, a long box will often suit a family of four or five. There will usually be space on the bars for at least one bike carrier, especially with T-track aluminium aerobars.

Price is important, but a roof box should be expected to last for many family holidays and weekends away, so it needs to be reliable. There is no such thing as a particular box for a particular car, but it makes sense to be able to open your tailgate fully, making sure that any tailgate spoiler doesn’t hit the back of the box.

Fitting & roof bars

Roof boxes are always fitted to a set of roof bars — a set of steel or aluminium bars going across the car, from one side to the other. Cars with factory-fitted longitudinal roof-rails will still need a set of roof bars going from rail to rail.

Apart from a few T-track-only models, any brand of roof box can be fitted to any brand of roof bars. A set of box-to-bar fittings is included with every roof box we sell, as is a set of straps to keep luggage in place. When a box is locked it can’t be removed from the bars, because the fittings that hold it on are tightened up inside the box.

  • If you also need roof bars, a package deal includes the bars and any adapters, and is usually the most economical way to buy both.
  • Aluminium T-track bars are quieter than square-edged steel bars and let you use the full length of the bar — useful if you also want to carry a bike.
  • Flush (“closed end”) bars are shorter than through bars, which can prevent you carrying bikes on the roof at the same time as a box.
  • If you already have aluminium bars, check whether you also need T-track roof box adapters. These are included as standard with some boxes, but are a vital accessory for others.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need anything else apart from roof bars and a roof box?

No. A set of box-to-standard-bar fittings is included with every roof box we sell, as is a set of straps to keep luggage in place. When a box is locked it can’t be removed from the bars because the fittings that hold it to the roof bars are tightened up inside the box, and the box will be locked.

I have a full-length box and it seems to overhang the bars a long way. Is this OK?

Yes. These boxes are designed so that approximately one third hangs out behind the rear bar, one third is supported by the roof bars, and one third overhangs the front bar.

Which side is left-hand opening and which side is right-hand opening?

Left-hand opening boxes open on the passenger side of a standard UK right-hand drive car, the “nearside”. Right-hand opening means the driver’s side, the “offside”.

Can I carry bikes on the roof as well as a roof box?

Yes — if you choose a box that leaves space on the roof bars, and bars that are long enough. Narrow or medium-width boxes are the usual choice when you also want to carry bikes, windsurfers or kayaks. T-track aluminium “through bars” make it easier to use the full length of the bar. Flush bars are shorter, which can get in the way of carrying bikes at the same time as a box.

Does a roof box open from both sides?

Dual side opening — the ability to open a box from either the left or the right — is particularly useful on full-width boxes, especially on taller vehicles. It is of less benefit on narrower boxes, which need to sit on one side of the car or the other and cannot then be reached from the opposite side. Check the product page for each box.
